# Thoughts about AI/LLMs

## LLMs are not well understood by the public

- The mechanism by which large language models or generative AI in general creates output is not well understood by most people. I believe that this interacts unfortunately with humans' habit of anthropomorphizing objects (technology, computers), especially when humans' interaction with that technology is mediated (or appears to be mediated) through language.
- Most LLMs serve the interests of a corporation such as Google or OpenAI. The models have been tuned by employees at these companies. The LLMs are not impartial "actors"; they have broad "goals" in the form of system prompts and various weights and parameters that are kept secret from the public. 
- I believe that sociological and critical analysis of these tools/models is often missing from conversations about them. The Luddites did not axiomatically despise technology; they opposed the concentration of resources and wealth into an ownership class. AI is often a tool of wage and labour suppression as productivity gains are not shared equally by society.

## LLMs are nondeterministic

- Given the same input several times, they tend to produce similar but not identical output. This is useful in some cases but deleterious when consistency is important (e.g., configuration files, computer scripts).

## LLMs are genuinely useful in some cases

- Although often presented as a tool for work, LLMs and generative AI can be used to create arts and crafts (although I believe artificial art is different from human art).
    - Writing prompts is a craft.
    - LLMs with very limited context windows and specialized prompts can create interesting output. Consider the ["I Forced a Bot"](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/i-forced-a-bot) meme, which, although created entirely by humans, emulated the style of a very incapable LLM.
