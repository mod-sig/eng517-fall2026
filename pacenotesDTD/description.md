# Pacenotes

Pacenotes comprise verbal descriptions of and instructions for navigating a
rally stage. A rally stage is a point-to-point course over various surfaces that
teams of competitors drive sequentially, competing for shortest time from start
to finish. Inside each rally car is a driver and codriver. The codriver reads each
note at the appropriate time to help the driver navigate the stage.

Because pacenotes are delivered in real time and in high-stakes situations, the
notes must be succinct and well disambiguated. But the notes are not completely
objective. Different drivers may perceive a single turn, for example, as
being of differing severity. And some drivers prefer more or less complex
information (e.g., five versus nine levels of turn severity). Codrivers determine
their own orthography because they convert writing to speech.

## Structure

I've created a simplified grammar of pacenotes for a single rally stage. Each
stage note has start instructions that preview the beginning of the stage as the
competitors wait on the start line. And each stage note also has instructions
that include what to do after crossing the finish line. Rally stages generally
employ a flying finish, so there are often additional turns after the finish.

Between the start and end instructions are different kinds of notes, each of
which describes a discrete portion of the stage (going straight for a certian
distance, surface changes, turns) and sometimes includes warnings or reference
points (e.g., "five right by house", "caution over crest"). The turn notes in
particular follow a strict syntax, often severity, direction, and modifiers.
This consistency allows drivers to focus on the mechanics of driving rather than
parsing the meaning of instructions or descriptions.
